# Verifier round 7: railroads_kaggle.mp4 (1080x1920, 30 fps, 1701 f, 19 scenes)

Method: read sbs_1..4, built per-scene contact sheets from every 4th frame of the whole video (every 8th for long scenes),
checked full-res frames 160, 700, 1340, 1640 and 1690, and measured CIELAB on every 4th frame with the caption band
(55-78 % height) masked. I compared these against the railroad target grid (row 2, image 1) and the channel references.
Shot table: rstory.ts. Captions: cap.ass.

## Colour: pink/purple tint
- Day scenes (c1, c2, c4-c9): mean L 0.55, a* +18.5, b* +11.5, lit chroma p90 57. 22 % of pixels have a pink/purple hue (290-15 deg, C* > 12). The day colour is close to target except that the canyon walls are pink; the reference walls are red-orange.
- Golden scenes (c11-c19): mean L 0.46, a* +21.0, b* -7.6, chroma p90 43. **73 % of pixels are pink/purple** (c12 81 %, c16 81 %, c19 89 %).
- Railroad target reference: a* +19, b* -1.9, 31 % pink/purple and 20 % blue (sky). The channel references: a* +14.5, b* -18.5, 56 % pink/purple (that is night/blue-hour content, not golden).
- **Verdict: YES, golden hour is pink/purple tinted.** It is about 2.4 times the target's share of pink/purple pixels, b* is 5.7 points too blue/magenta, and chroma p90 is 43 against the target's 53. SKILL.md asks for a peach horizon (#ffd08a), lit key toward #FFB36B and violet-blue only in shade. Our lit surfaces are mauve too, so the tint is in the key/grade, not just the shadows.

## Corrupted frames
- No tiled strips, blank frames or broken frames found in the 426 sampled frames.
- Double images appear only at the three "fly" transitions: 293-302 (c3 to c4), 951-960 (c10 to c11) and 1544-1553 (c18 to c19). These are 10 f cross-dissolves. They are intended and not corrupt, but the "fly" kind is only a crossfade.
- Ink artefact that looks like corruption: at frame 160 (c2) and 1600-1700 (c19), box edges of fogged canyon blocks show as crisp dark wireframe lines over the fog.

- **c2 (90-218)**: the aerial follows a train on the RIGHT (rust) track. Screen direction is correct and the reveal of the canyon drop is good. But the line "so deep you can't see the bottom" (178-212) plays over a fully visible, brightly lit orange-red canyon floor, which contradicts the caption. (item 2)
- **c3 (219-297)**, office: good. The boss is centred, there is a chart and a "CLICKETY" paper, and the push-in is fine.
- **c4 (298-352)**: the engineer is on his track and the sawn gap is visible as a black notch behind him at 312-330. OK.
- **c5 (353-484)**: the boss shot and the gap insert at 400-429 match the caption "gap" at 401, and the face turns purple on cue at 455-466. Good timing. But the "finger-wide" gap is 0.2 m (gapR 0.2): the void is about 3 times the rail-head width, closer to a hand's width. (item 3)
- **c6 (485-524)**: two-shot, boss on screen-left and engineer on screen-right, both on the right track. The boss's face stays purple, which is good continuity. OK.
- **c7 (525-576)** "won't close a single one": the gap is visible bottom-left at 528-548, then leaves the frame as the camera glides. From 552 to 576, during "SINGLE ONE.", there is no gap in frame. (item 4)
- **c8 (577-691)**: the rival is on the LEFT track, with the blue truss screen-left and the rust bridge screen-right, which is consistent. The rail has no gap, which is good for "perfectly smooth". There is no rail-laying action. Acceptable.
- **c9 (692-823)**: the left train is correct, on the blue bridge screen-left. The gap goes from 0.2 m to 0.1 m, but the black void looks the same width in the first and last frames, so the shrinking cannot be seen. The sleepers read as stairs. (items 9, 10)
- **c13 (1077-1138)** "you can fit a coin in them": the coin starts as a yellow dot on the engineer's forehead (1088), drops to hand height by about 1112, and then hovers in mid-air about 0.4 m above the rail until 1136. It never goes into the gap. (item 11)
- **c14 (1139-1208)**: in the paper insert (1139-1165) the sheet is about 4 times the rail height and stands upright behind the rail end. It is not slid into the joint, and the joint is hidden by the foreground rail block. The boss "thrilled" shot is fine. (item 12)

The rail is a giant lavender slab with orange stains, which is abstract and unreadable. ## Coherence and readiness
- **First frame (0)**: the engineer on his track under a blue sky. Coherent as an opening, but the hook action (sawing) is missing.
- **Last frame (1700)**: the blue train on the buckled blue bridge. Coherent with the final caption "IT.", but there is no settle or payoff beat.
- **Scene order**: c1 to c19 matches the caption order. The office scenes are at c3 and c10 and golden hour runs from c11 to c19, as specified. Left/right track identity is consistent in screen direction throughout. Captions are aligned to within 1-5 f of the cuts.
- **Ready as final video: NO.** There are no technical defects. It fails because about half the narrative beats (items 1, 6, 11, 12, 14, 18) cannot be read from the picture, c16 shows the wrong subject, golden hour is clearly magenta-tinted against the target (item 20), and the canyon reads as a city of striped boxes (item 21). The minimum to ship is items 1, 6, 7, 11, 12, 14, 18 and 20.
