In nineteen seventy-one, a man hijacked a plane, jumped out with two hundred thousand dollars, and was never seen again. He bought a ticket under the name Dan Cooper. Calm, polite, in a suit and tie. Mid-flight, he handed a note to a flight attendant. It said he had a bomb. He showed her the wires in his briefcase. Then he made his demands: two hundred thousand dollars, and four parachutes. On the ground, the money was handed over. He let the passengers go. Then he ordered the pilots to take off again, and to fly low and slow toward Mexico. Somewhere over the forests of Washington, in the rain and the dark, he opened the rear stairs and leapt into the night. They searched for years. The FBI never found him. The only trace was a bundle of his cash, dug up by a boy near a river, in nineteen eighty. It remains the only unsolved hijacking in commercial aviation history.
