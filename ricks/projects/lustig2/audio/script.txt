A man sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice. Built for the eighteen eighty-nine World's Fair, it was only meant to stand twenty years, and many Parisians already hated it. In nineteen twenty-five, Victor Lustig invited scrap dealers to a Paris hotel, the Crillon, posing as a government official. On fake stationery, he told them the tower was rusting, too costly to repair, and must be sold for scrap. All seven thousand tons of its iron. A dealer named Andre Poisson bought it, and handed over the cash. You see, the tower was never his to sell, but Poisson was too embarrassed to call the police. Lustig left Paris within the hour. So six months later, the con man came back, found new dealers, and sold the Eiffel Tower again. This time, someone went to the police. He fled to America, and borrowed fifty thousand dollars from Al Capone, only to give it back and earn his trust. Capone rewarded his honesty with five thousand. In the end, the law caught him, and he got twenty years in Alcatraz. And the Eiffel Tower? Still standing.
