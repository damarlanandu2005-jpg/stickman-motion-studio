One man was struck by lightning seven times, and survived every single one. Roy Sullivan was a park ranger in Virginia. The first bolt hit him through his leg and blew off his big toenail. Years later, lightning hit him while he was driving. Then again, in his own front yard. Then it set his hair on fire. Twice. People began to back away from him when storms rolled in. Even his wife was struck once, in the yard beside him. Seven times, the sky tried to kill him. Seven times, he got back up, burned but breathing. Guinness World Records says nobody has been struck more, and people called him the human lightning rod. No one could explain it. The odds of being hit even once are tiny. The odds of seven are almost beyond counting. In the end, after dodging the sky for decades, Roy died in nineteen eighty-three, at seventy-one. Lightning never got him.
