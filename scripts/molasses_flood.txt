TITLE: The Great Molasses Flood

NARRATION:
In nineteen nineteen, a wave of molasses killed twenty-one people.
In the north end of Boston stood a giant steel tank, five stories tall, holding two and a half million gallons of molasses for making alcohol. It groaned. It leaked. The company just painted it brown to hide the drips.
On a warm January afternoon, it burst. A wall of molasses, twenty-five feet high, swept through the streets at thirty-five miles an hour.
It was faster than a person could run. It crushed buildings, swallowed horses, and tore a firehouse off its foundation. Twenty-one people died in syrup.
The cleanup took weeks. The harbor ran brown until summer.
And for decades after, on hot days, locals swore the whole neighborhood still smelled of molasses.
