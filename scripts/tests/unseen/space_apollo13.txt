Apollo 13. Two days from Earth, an oxygen tank explodes. The crew crawls into the tiny lunar module, a lifeboat built for two, now holding three. Then comes a worse problem. Their air filters are full, and the carbon dioxide in the cabin is climbing. Spare filters exist, but they are square, and the lunar module's slots are round. On the ground, engineers dump a pile of the exact junk on board onto a table: a plastic bag, a cardboard cover, a sock, and duct tape. Step by step they talk the astronauts through building an adapter. The square filter is taped into the round hole. The numbers drop. They breathe, and they go home.
