Why do geese fly in a V? It is not for looks. A flying goose pushes air down and back, and behind its wingtip a swirl of air rises. Fly in that rising air and it lifts you, like a free ride. So the second goose tucks just behind and beside the first, in the lift. The third does the same behind the second. The leader gets no help and works the hardest. So after a while it drops back, and another goose takes the front. They honk too, calling encouragement to the ones ahead. Together they can fly much farther than any one of them alone.
