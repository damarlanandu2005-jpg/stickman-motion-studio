October 1415. A tired English army, sick and starving, is trapped in a muddy field in France. Facing them are twelve thousand armoured French knights, with maybe six thousand English archers. The night before, it rains hard. In the morning the field is a swamp. The French charge on horses and on foot, heavy in steel. Their horses panic when arrows fall like hail, and slide in the mud. Knights fall. The ones behind trip over them, and cannot get up. Light English archers drop their bows, grab axes and swords, and move through the pile. By afternoon, the French lose thousands. The English lose a few hundred. The mud was a weapon.
