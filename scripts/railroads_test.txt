TITLE: Why Perfect Railroads Don't Work

NARRATION:
He sawed gaps into a railroad every thirty-nine feet. See, two companies are racing to build a railroad across a canyon so deep you can't see the bottom. And whoever finishes first gets the whole country's business. He's the youngest engineer on the job. And when his boss sees a finger wide gap between every single rail, his face turns purple. You're wrecking my railroad. But he won't close a single one. The other one lays rails end to end, perfectly smooth, and finishes first. Their trains glide across the canyon without a sound, while his go clickety clack over every gap. The newspapers start calling it the Clickety Clack Line, and everyone switches to the quiet trains. Then summer comes, and something weird happens. His gaps start shrinking in May. You can fit a coin in them by July. Barely a sheet of paper, and his boss is thrilled. See, even your gaps know you were wrong. Meanwhile, on the perfect railroad, the rails started groaning in the afternoon sun, pushing against each other like they want out. On the hottest day anyone can remember. His gaps finally close. Rail touching rail. Halfway across the canyon, the perfect railroad kicks sideways like a snake, right as a train rolls onto it.
